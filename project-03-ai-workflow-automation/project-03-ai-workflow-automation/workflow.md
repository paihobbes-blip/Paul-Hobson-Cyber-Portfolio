# 🧠 Workflow Design

## Objective

Turn messy, unstructured information into a usable triage output without losing context or accountability.

---

## 1. Intake

The workflow starts with raw input such as:
- notes
- event fragments
- observations
- copied text
- mixed narrative and facts

At this stage, the information may be:
- incomplete
- repetitive
- out of order
- ambiguous
- emotionally noisy

---

## 2. First-Pass AI Structuring

AI is used to perform an initial pass that:
- extracts key facts
- groups related points
- identifies possible risks
- highlights actions or next steps
- suggests a structure for the final output

This stage is useful for reducing chaos, but it is **not treated as truth**.

---

## 3. Human Review

The human reviewer checks:
- what is clearly supported
- what is inferred
- what is missing
- what needs rewording
- whether priorities make sense

This stage is where judgement matters most.

---

## 4. Output Assembly

The final output is organised into a structure such as:
- summary
- key facts
- risks
- actions
- validation notes

This produces something another person can quickly read and act on.

---

## 5. Reflection / Improvement

The workflow can then be improved by asking:
- Which AI outputs were useful?
- Which outputs were inaccurate?
- What should be validated more carefully next time?
- What structure was easiest to act on?

---

## Why This Matters

This pattern is highly relevant in environments where:
- people are overloaded
- inputs are messy
- errors matter
- decisions need to be documented

That includes cyber security, operations, investigations, and incident handling.
